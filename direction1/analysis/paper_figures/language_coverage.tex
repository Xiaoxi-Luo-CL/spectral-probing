We select 24 target languages from Universal Dependencies v2.17
\citep{nivre-etal-2020-universal} according to three criteria.
First, we stratify by \textbf{resource level}: eight high-resource languages
(${>}$5{,}000 annotated sentences) serve as upper-bound transfer targets,
eight mid-resource languages (1{,}000--2{,}000 sentences) represent an
intermediate regime, and eight low-resource languages (200--1{,}000 sentences)
stress-test transfer to genuinely data-scarce settings.
Second, we stratify by \textbf{multilingual model support}: within each resource
tier, four languages are covered by mBERT and XLM-R and four are not,
allowing us to separate the effect of pre-training coverage from that of
spectral transfer.
Third, we maximise \textbf{typological diversity}: the set spans ten language
families (Indo-European, Turkic, Sino-Tibetan, Dravidian, Austronesian,
Koreanic, Japonic, Uralic, Mande, and Mayan--Quichean), both Roman and
non-Roman scripts, and a wide range of morphological and syntactic features
including agglutination, ergativity, polysynthesis, tonal systems, and
non-SVO word orders.
Table~\ref{tab:languages} lists all 24 languages together with their
UD treebank, resource tier, model support, and primary linguistic motivation.
